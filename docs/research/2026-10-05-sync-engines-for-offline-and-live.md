Sync engines for offline editing and live updates on Supabase: options and costs

Observed October 5, 2026, on branch `docs/platform-map-1cmyxm`. Research for Wayfinder ticket #121; it writes no application code. It re-checks the pricing and capability claims in [2026-10-03-v1-architecture-research.md](2026-10-03-v1-architecture-research.md) (sections "Offline editing is a local database plus synchronization" and "Figma-style responsiveness") and extends them to ElectricSQL, Zero, TanStack DB, Dexie Cloud and the Yjs server options. Sources are the vendors' own pricing pages, docs and source code (fetched from vendor sites, raw.githubusercontent.com and the npm CDN), the MDN browser-compat data package, and WebKit's blog. GitHub API access to third-party repos was blocked in this session, so repo facts come from rendered pages and raw files. Secondary write-ups were not used as evidence. Auth terms (JWT, JWKS, RLS, secret key) are defined in [2026-10-05-better-auth-with-supabase.md](2026-10-05-better-auth-with-supabase.md); this file uses "pattern (a)" from that doc to mean Better Auth owns identity and only the SvelteKit server talks to Supabase.

One term used throughout. A **sync engine** keeps a copy of the user's records on the device, lets the app read and write that copy while offline, and later reconciles it with Postgres. "Live updates" is the separate job of telling other open screens that something changed; Supabase Realtime does that on its own, but it does not store anything.

## Short answer

The 2026-10-03 doc's prices are still right (PowerSync Pro "From $49/month", RxDB Pro "From $99/month billed annually", Supabase Pro "from $25/month") and its conflict-semantics claims hold: no engine on this list gives Trident's "keep both versions of a contested field" rule for free; every one of them defaults to a form of last-write-wins or server-wins and leaves per-field retention, idempotent replay and permission checks to the app's write endpoint. What changed since October 3 is listed in section 9: PowerSync's free plan is now quantified and is deprovisioned after 7 idle days, its Sync Rules were deprecated on 2026-09-22 in favour of Sync Streams, its separate attachments package is deprecated in favour of a built-in helper, and electric-sql.com now redirects to electric.ax, where the company has repositioned around agents and prices Electric Cloud as pay-as-you-go.

Recommendation, **medium confidence**: keep PowerSync as the first engine to prototype, on its **free** plan, with the Supabase Pro plan as the only paid service ($25/month, well under the $75 ceiling). The free plan's 2 GB synced per month and 50 peak concurrent clients fit a field team of surveyors if photos and video stay out of the sync stream (PowerSync's own attachments helper does exactly that). Budget the Pro step ($49, giving $74 with Supabase Pro, or $78 if the IPv4 add-on turns out to be needed) as the trigger that ends the free period, not as a day-one cost. Under Better Auth pattern (a) PowerSync is the only engine here that works without a browser-side Supabase token: it verifies any JWKS, and its uploads go to the app's own endpoint. RxDB's Supabase plugin and y-supabase both drive supabase-js from the browser and therefore need a token Supabase can verify, which pattern (a) does not have. Zero is excluded on its own statement that it "does not support offline writes". For shared paragraphs, use Yjs with y-indexeddb on the device and persist updates server-side; the cheapest honest option is a self-hosted Hocuspocus (MIT) behind Better Auth, because the hosted alternatives (Liveblocks $30/month, Tiptap Cloud $59/month) consume the budget and y-supabase is a 10-star alpha.

## 8. Living with Supabase Realtime and Better Auth

- Supabase Realtime quotas: Free "200 included" peak connections and "2 Million included" messages; Pro "500 included, then $10 per 1000" and "5 Million included, then $2.50 per Million" ([pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Broadcast does not replay missed messages ([earlier doc](2026-10-03-v1-architecture-research.md), unchanged). Presence and cursors stay on Realtime whichever engine wins; PowerSync, RxDB and a Dexie outbox each deliver saved rows through their own channel, so Realtime is never the recovery path.
- Pattern (a) has no browser Supabase token. PowerSync needs none (section 1). RxDB's Supabase plugin, y-supabase and private Realtime channels all do; the Realtime gap is the one already logged in [the Better Auth doc](2026-10-05-better-auth-with-supabase.md), and the record engine choice does not widen it as long as PowerSync or a Dexie outbox is used.
- Supabase Free "projects are paused after 1 week of inactivity" and PowerSync Free instances are deprovisioned after 7 idle days; a V1 pilot therefore needs Supabase Pro ($25) plus traffic to keep the free PowerSync instance alive, or a scheduled client connection.
