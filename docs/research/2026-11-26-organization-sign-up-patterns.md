How big products handle organizations, email domains, invites and the first person to sign up

Observed November 26, 2026. Research for "Decide how people, companies and places are saved" ([#357](https://github.com/layerdbiz/tridentcubed/issues/357)), so its `/grill-with-docs` session starts from facts. Justin's question, from #357: when the first person signs up with a company email (name@company.com), do they become that organization's admin or owner straight away, and how do Google, Cloudflare, Microsoft and the like handle organizations, domains, invites, roles and claiming an organization? This file changes nothing in the app and decides nothing. Sources are each vendor's own help centre, admin docs and API reference, and Better Auth's own docs and source. Secondary write-ups were not used. The key quotes for Google, Cloudflare, Slack, Linear, Notion and Vercel were checked again against the live pages on the same day; Microsoft's came back as full page text.

Terms used below. **Owner** or **top admin** is whatever the product calls the role that can do everything, including managing the other admins (Super Admin, Global Administrator, Super Administrator, Primary Owner, Owner). **Domain joining** is letting someone join an organization because their email ends in that organization's domain, with no invite. **Domain proof** is showing you control the domain itself, nearly always by adding a DNS TXT record, which is a step beyond proving you can read one mailbox at it. **Claiming** is a company taking control of accounts or workspaces its people made before the company itself signed up.

## Short answer

1. **Yes: whoever creates an organization becomes its top admin at once, in every product read.** Google: "If you signed up for Google Workspace, you're an administrator." Microsoft: the person who signed up "is a Global Administrator automatically." Cloudflare: "you are automatically assigned the Super Administrator role." Slack: "When you create a workspace, you'll become the Workspace Primary Owner." Linear, Notion, GitHub and Vercel imply the same without saying it in one sentence. The one exception is Microsoft's self-service sign-up, which makes an organization with **no** admin until someone proves the domain.
2. **Joining because of the email domain is never on by default.** An organization's own admin switches it on and names the domains (Slack, Linear, Notion, Microsoft; Vercel has it as an API field). People who join that way get the ordinary member role, not admin (Microsoft: "a regular member"; Notion: "as members", except on Notion's Free plan, where they become owners). GitHub, Cloudflare and Google keep joining invite-only.
3. **The collaboration tools do not ask for domain proof before domain joining.** Slack, Linear and Notion trust the verified email people sign up with. Notion's one guard: a domain can be added only once someone with that domain is already in the workspace. Domain proof by DNS is a separate, usually paid or enterprise step, and it is what lets a company block unofficial organizations on its domain and claim existing accounts. One domain belongs to one organization ("A domain name can only be verified in one directory", Microsoft).
4. **Claiming is never silent.** Google asks each person to transfer their account; Notion gives existing owners 14 days' notice and never shows the company their content; Vercel archives or lets each person choose; GitHub makes brand-new accounts instead. When the first person to sign up was not really the boss, Slack moves ownership only at the request of "a senior staff member", and Google makes whoever later proves the domain "the sole admin".
5. **Personal addresses (gmail.com and the like) come in only by invite, as outsiders with narrower access.** Notion refuses gmail.com and outlook.com as allowed domains; Google won't let anyone found a team on one; Cloudflare bans them from its domain sign-in. Invites expire after 7 days (GitHub, Vercel) or 30 days (Slack), except Microsoft's, which never do.
6. **Every product protects the last owner and recommends two.** Microsoft: "A Global Administrator can't remove their own Global Administrator assignment"; Vercel: "You can't leave a team if you are the last remaining owner". Handover is always: add the new owner first, then remove the old one.
7. **Better Auth, our sign-in library, already does the creator rule, invites and the last-admin guard, and nothing about domains.** Whoever creates an organization becomes its member with `creatorRole` (`admin` in our app). Invites are built in (48 hours by default, accepted once the person has signed in with the invited email). The last holder of the creator role cannot leave or drop it. Its organization plugin has no domain joining, no domain proof, no join requests and no list of public email domains, in 1.7.28 or the 1.8 beta. Its SSO plugin can prove a domain by DNS and join people by it, but only for Google and Microsoft sign-ins through a registered single sign-on provider, never for email codes, and with a role name our database refuses. So domain joining stays the app's own sign-in rule, as it is today ([section 8](#8-what-better-auth-gives-out-of-the-box)).

## 4. Personal addresses and outsiders

- **Public email domains are refused as company domains.** Notion: "Widely-used public domains such as gmail.com and outlook.com can't be set as allowed email domains", and people on them, or on school addresses such as nyu.edu, "won't be able to auto-join other workspaces" ([enterprise guide](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/the-ultimate-quickstart-guide-to-notion-for-enterprise), [switch workspaces](https://www.notion.com/help/create-delete-and-switch-workspaces)). Google: "You can't sign up with an address at a public web host like gmail.com or yahoo.com" ([Essentials setup](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/getting-started/editions/set-up-essentials-for-your-domain)). Cloudflare bans them from domain sign-in, and university domains too, because they "include students". Slack, Linear, GitHub and Vercel say nothing either way.
- **Outsiders are guests with narrower access, by invite only.** Microsoft has a formal "Guest" user type, "a user who isn't considered internal to the company, such as an external collaborator, partner, or customer", and signs Gmail guests in through Google ([user properties](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/external-id/user-properties), [redemption](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/external-id/redemption-experience)). Slack has single- and multi-channel guests, Linear and Notion guests per team or page, GitHub "outside collaborators", Vercel viewers and contributors. Google and Cloudflare have no guest member: Google shares files externally, Cloudflare scopes a role to chosen resources.

## 6. Roles

Every product has the same three layers, under different names: an **owner** layer (Super Admin, Global Administrator, Super Administrator, Primary Owner and Owners, Owner), an **admin** layer that runs people and settings but cannot touch the owners or billing (Cloudflare "Administrator": "Cannot manage members nor billing profile"; Slack Workspace Admins; Linear Admin), and an ordinary **member** layer, plus **guests** for outsiders. Slack is the only one with a single named Primary Owner above several equal Owners; Notion, GitHub, Vercel and Cloudflare have equal owners. Handover is the same everywhere: "you must add a new owner, ensure that the billing information is updated, and then remove yourself" (GitHub, [transfer](https://docs.github.com/en/organizations/managing-organization-settings/transferring-organization-ownership)); "promote the new Owner, then demote or remove the original Owner" (Vercel, [managing members](https://vercel.com/docs/rbac/managing-team-members)).

## 7. How Trident Cubed works today (origin/next, 2026-11-26)

- One organization, Trident Cubed. Roles Admin, Manager and Team; a User with no membership is a Guest; the Super admin is a flag above every organization ([ADR 0003](../adr/0003-access-is-organization-membership-checked-in-one-place.md) and its amendments, [glossary](../trident/GLOSSARY.md)).
- Memberships are made only by the server's sign-in rule (`grantAccess` in `apps/app/src/lib/server/access.ts`), at every sign-in: a private access grant first, then the organization's email domain (Team role), otherwise Guest. The email domain "stays set in the database only, never from the app" (ADR 0003, amendment of 2026-11-24). In the terms above, Trident Cubed's own organization already has **domain joining switched on, set by the platform rather than by the organization's admin, with no DNS proof**, which is the Slack, Linear and Notion pattern.
- Better Auth's organization plugin holds the organizations and memberships, with the three roles, `creatorRole: 'admin'` and `allowUserToCreateOrganization: false`; its own HTTP routes are closed, so nobody can create an organization or invite through it (`apps/app/src/lib/server/auth.ts`).
- Customers, facilities and personnel are records inside Trident Cubed's organization, not organizations of their own (#357 body). #357's notes, not decided: customers become organizations later, people join one by their company email's domain, Gmail-type addresses need an invite, and an organization is a Customer only when a Trident Cubed Admin marks it or it pays for a project.

### What the app would build itself

Who may create a company's organization and become its admin; the list of public email domains; domain joining for email-code sign-ins (today's `grantAccess`); domain proof for companies without single sign-on, if wanted (the pattern is small: a token, a TXT record at a fixed name, a DNS lookup); what happens when one domain matches two organizations; join requests and approval; handover as a flow; the invite landing page; invite rights on the admin role and a Row Level Security policy for the `invitation` table.

## 9. What this means for Trident Cubed

Facts that bear on the session, not decisions.

- **Trident's case is closer to a service company inviting its clients than to Slack's open sign-up.** Trident's Admins already know each customer and its contacts, and #357 says an organization becomes a Customer only by an Admin's mark or a paid project. Products that sell to companies through a person (Slack Enterprise domain claims through support, Notion's sales-assisted claiming, Slack moving the Primary Owner only on a senior staff member's request) keep a human check at the point where a company's control changes hands.
- **"First signer is admin" is the universal default, and every product backs it with a way to fix it later**: more than one owner, a last-owner guard, and a route for the real company to take over (domain proof or support). If Trident copies the default, it needs the fix too; the Super admin and Trident Cubed's Admins could be that route, without any DNS step.
- **Domain joining without DNS proof is normal**, as long as it is opt-in per organization, gives only the lowest role, refuses public email domains, and (Notion's guard) accepts only a domain that someone already in the organization has. Trident Cubed's own organization already works this way.
- **DNS proof is an enterprise feature everywhere** and needs the company's IT person to add a record. It is the only defence against a rival organization being made on the same domain, but with Trident's Admins in the loop the same protection can come from "one domain, one organization" enforced by the app.
- **Better Auth makes either answer to "who runs it" cheap.** One server call creates an organization for a given User and makes them its Admin, with the last-Admin guard already in place, whether the app calls it at that User's sign-up or when a Trident Cubed Admin picks them. Domain joining, public-domain refusal and "one domain, one organization" are the app's own rules either way, in the same sign-in rule that already does it for Trident Cubed.
- **Leads captured before sign-up** (#357) match how Slack and Vercel treat requests: an approved-domain request is approved automatically (Slack), otherwise an owner approves (Vercel). A saved lead could be matched at sign-up to the organization its company already has.

## 10. Questions for the `/grill-with-docs #357` session

Yes/no where possible; the pattern above is noted beside each.

1. When someone signs up with a company email and Trident has **no** organization for that domain yet, do they stay a Guest until a Trident Cubed Admin sets the organization up, rather than the app making one for them? (Products make one at once; Trident's Admins already know their customers.)
2. When a company's first person signs up, do they become its **Admin straight away**? (Every product: yes. The alternative is that a Trident Cubed Admin names each company's first Admin, like Slack's and Notion's sales-assisted flows.)
3. Does each organization keep **at least one Admin**, with handover done by adding the new Admin before the old one leaves, and can the Super admin always step in? (Every product.)
4. Is **joining by email domain switched on per organization** by its Admin (or a Trident Cubed Admin), rather than on for everyone? (Slack, Linear, Notion, Microsoft: opt-in.)
5. Do people who join by domain always get the **Team role**, never Manager or Admin? (Microsoft and Notion paid plans: ordinary member.)
6. Does the app keep a list of **public email domains** (gmail.com, outlook.com, yahoo.com, icloud.com and the like) that can never be an organization's domain, so those people only join by invite? (Notion, Google, Cloudflare.)
7. Is **one domain, one organization** a rule, so nobody can make a second organization for a domain that already has one? (Google, Microsoft, Notion.)
8. Do **invites expire**, and after how long: 2 days (Better Auth's default), 7 days (GitHub, Vercel) or 30 days (Slack)?
9. Is **DNS proof of a domain left out** for now, with a Trident Cubed Admin's say-so standing in for it? (DNS proof is a paid or enterprise step in every product.)
